# Otto-Barton 1997 recombination modifier

```
Theory: Otto-Barton 1997 evolution of
  recombination as a modifier that
  removes limits to natural selection
Original paper (year): Otto SP, Barton
  NH. 1997. The evolution of
  recombination: removing the limits to
  natural selection. Genetics 147(2):
  879-906 (1 October 1997).
  DOI 10.1093/genetics/147.2.879
  PMID 9335621 PMCID PMC1208206.
  EuropePMC HTML opened
  (https://europepmc.org/article/MED/9335621):
  Otto SP, Department of Zoology,
  University of British Columbia,
  Vancouver; Barton NH; 01 Oct 1997;
  Genetics 147(2):879-906; DOI; PMID;
  PMC; abstract. One of the oldest
  hypotheses for the advantage of
  recombination: recombination places
  beneficial mutations that arise in
  different individuals onto the same
  chromosome; without recombination,
  one beneficial allele is doomed to
  extinction. A modifier of
  recombination is modelled when
  beneficial alleles are segregating at
  other loci. Modifiers that increase
  recombination raise the fixation
  probability of beneficial mutants and
  then hitchhike as those mutants rise.
  Strength of selection on a modifier
  is proportional to lambda^2 S delta r
  / r when linkage is tight and to
  lambda^2 S^3 delta r / N when linkage
  is loose (lambda = beneficial
  mutation rate per genome per
  generation in a population of size N;
  S = average mutant effect; r =
  average recombination rate; delta r =
  the modification). Selection for
  recombination is substantial only if
  there is tight linkage or if many
  loci are under directional selection
  during rapid evolutionary change.
  Distinct from filled Felsenstein 1974
  (ratchet advantage of recombination;
  not the beneficial-modifier
  hitchhike). Distinct from filled Hill
  and Robertson 1966. Distinct from
  filled Maynard Smith 1978 two-fold
  cost (does not map). Distinct from
  Barton 1995 this wave (fixation
  limits, not evolution of a
  recombination modifier). Distinct
  from filled Barton 1979 / 1983 /
  1986. Barton 2000 is a review (not
  used). Otto and Whitton 2000 is a
  review (not used).
One-sentence prediction: A modifier
  that increases recombination raises
  the fixation probability of
  beneficial mutants and hitchhikes
  with them, but that selection is
  substantial only under tight linkage
  or many loci under directional
  selection.
Assumptions: a sexual population with
  meiosis and a recombination modifier;
  grafted clones skip meiosis; a
  cultivar panel is not N.
Which fail here: grafted clones skip
  meiosis, so a recombination modifier
  cannot hitchhike between cuttings.
  A cultivar collection is not N.
  Clone IBD fakes linkage. Dual
  domestication is two origins. Dong
  2023 color / sex / flavor are few
  large-effect loci, not many loci
  under directional selection.
Modern data / statistic: whether
  recombination after clone + IBD
  filters is an Otto-Barton modifier
  walk (selection for more r only if
  linkage is tight or many loci are
  under directional selection), versus
  clone IBD or two origins.
Dataset: Dong et al. 2023 Science
  (DOI 10.1126/science.add8655). Wild
  sylvestris after clone + IBD filters.
  Use as the recombination-modifier
  contrast, not as a published grape
  1997 analysis. Do not invent a grape
  recombination-modifier paper.
What would falsify it: recombination
  is not an Otto-Barton modifier walk
  after clone filter (no meiosis, or
  two origins, or few large-effect
  loci). Ratchet advantage remains
  Felsenstein 1974. Linkage limits
  remain Hill-Robertson 1966 and
  Barton 1995. Two-fold cost remains
  Maynard Smith 1978 (does not map).
What we will not claim: that
  Felsenstein 1974 is this paper
  (already filled). That Hill and
  Robertson 1966 is this paper. That
  Barton 1995 is this paper. That
  Barton 2000 is the source (review).
  That Otto and Whitton 2000 is the
  source (review). That Dong 2023
  estimated a recombination modifier.
  That VviAGL11 is the sex locus.
```
