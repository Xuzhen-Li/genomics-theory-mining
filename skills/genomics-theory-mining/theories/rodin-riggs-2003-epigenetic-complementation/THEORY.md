# Rodin-Riggs 2003 epigenetic complementation

```
Theory: Rodin-Riggs 2003 epigenetic
  complementation (EC) of new
  duplicates
Original paper (year): Rodin SN, Riggs
  AD. 2003. Epigenetic silencing may
  aid evolution by gene duplication.
  J Mol Evol 56(6):718-729 (1 June
  2003).
  DOI 10.1007/s00239-002-2446-6
  PMID 12911035. EuropePMC HTML opened
  (https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12911035):
  Rodin SN, Theoretical Biology
  Department, Beckman Research
  Institute of the City of Hope,
  Duarte, CA; Riggs AD; 01 Jun 2003;
  Journal of Molecular Evolution
  56(6):718-729; DOI; PMID; abstract.
  Loss-versus-gain dilemma after
  duplication: most new duplicates
  become pseudogenes because
  degenerative mutations are much more
  frequent than advantageous ones.
  Suggestion: epigenetic silencing of
  duplicates can shift that
  equilibrium toward functional
  divergence. Functional young
  duplicates are more frequent in
  organisms with cytosine methylation
  (human, mouse, Arabidopsis) than in
  organisms without methylated genomes
  (yeast, Drosophila, C. elegans). The
  classic model (identical expression
  of new duplicates) is extended to
  tissue- and/or stage-complementary
  epigenetic silencing, which exposes
  each copy to negative selection and
  protects it from pseudogenization.
  That silencing (i) enhances evolution
  of new functions, particularly in
  small populations, (ii) is consistent
  with subfunctionalization when
  complementary degenerative mutations
  hit different subfunctions, and
  (iii) may cooperate with the DDC
  process. Distinct from filled Force
  1999 (DDC complementary degeneration
  of regulatory elements; not
  epigenetic silencing). Distinct from
  filled Walsh 1995 (rho-S neo versus
  null). Distinct from filled Lynch
  and Conery 2000 (birth-and-silencing
  rate). Distinct from filled He and
  Zhang 2005 SNF. Distinct from filled
  Ohno 1970 gene balance. Innan and
  Kondrashov 2010 and Zhang 2003 were
  not used (reviews).
One-sentence prediction: Tissue- or
  stage-complementary epigenetic
  silencing exposes each new duplicate
  to purifying selection, protecting it
  from becoming a pseudogene and
  cooperating with DDC, especially in
  small populations and methylated
  genomes.
Assumptions: a recent duplicate pair
  that can be silenced in a
  complementary tissue or stage;
  palaeo-hexaploid ohnologs are not
  that pair; clone SVs are not
  epigenetic silencing.
Which fail here: Vitis ohnologs are
  old palaeo-hexaploid pairs, not a
  recent methylated duplicate walking
  EC. Clone SVs (Zhou 2019 /
  Carbonell-Bejerano 2017) change dose
  without a new epigenetic partition.
  A cultivar panel is not a small
  methylated Ne. Dual domestication is
  two origins. Tandem NLR arrays are
  not the paper's EC class.
Modern data / statistic: whether
  palaeo-hexaploid ohnolog pairs after
  clone filters are a Rodin-Riggs EC
  series (complementary epigenetic
  silencing protecting both copies),
  versus DDC or gene balance.
Dataset: Jaillon et al. 2007 Nature
  449:463-467 (DOI 10.1038/nature06148).
  Three ancestral genomes; no recent
  WGD. Dong et al. 2023 Science
  (DOI 10.1126/science.add8655) as the
  wild-versus-cultivar Ne contrast
  after clone + IBD filters. Use as
  the EC contrast, not as a published
  grape Rodin-Riggs analysis. Do not
  invent a grape epigenetic-
  complementation paper.
What would falsify it: ohnolog pairs
  are not a Rodin-Riggs EC series after
  clone filter (DDC alone, or gene
  balance, or two origins). DDC remains
  Force 1999. Neo versus null remains
  Walsh 1995. Birth-and-silencing
  remains Lynch-Conery 2000.
What we will not claim: that Force 1999
  is this paper (already filled). That
  Walsh 1995 is this paper. That
  Jaillon 2007 scored epigenetic
  complementation. That a clone SV is
  epigenetic silencing. That VviAGL11
  is the sex locus.
```
